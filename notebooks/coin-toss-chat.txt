**The short answer: No, you cannot freely mix them. You need one environment.**

From `books/system/doc/acl2-doc.html`:
> "ACL2(r) and ACL2 have **different underlying theories**, and books certified in one system **may not be included in the other**."

The `may not` is not a maybe — it's a category warning. The theories diverge because ACL2(r) adds nonclassical axioms (transfer principle, standardness predicate, etc.) that are inconsistent with what some classical proofs assume.

**The three cases:**

| Cert origin | Loaded by standard ACL2 | Loaded by ACL2(r) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard ACL2 cert | ✅ Always works | ⚠️ Usually works, but **not guaranteed** — some books are marked `non-acl2r` and explicitly excluded from ACL2(r) builds |
| ACL2(r) cert | ❌ Fails — missing nonclassical axioms/symbols | ✅ Works |

The `non-acl2r` cert_param (from `books/build/doc.html`) exists as a first-class mechanism to tag standard books that **must not** be loaded under ACL2(r). So ACL2 itself knows some standard books are incompatible with ACL2(r).

**For the proof project:** You need a single ACL2(r) environment. You do not maintain two parallel cert trees. The workflow is:

1. Build `saved_acl2r`
2. Certify your needed books under ACL2(r) — including the standard arithmetic books (they're compatible) **and** the nonstd books
3. The `arithmetic/binomial` and similar books will recertify cleanly under ACL2(r); their proofs don't assume classical-only axioms
4. All cert files in play are produced by ACL2(r)

There's no supported "mix" mode — the `.cert` file embeds which features (`:non-standard-analysis` or not) were active, and the loader checks this.
